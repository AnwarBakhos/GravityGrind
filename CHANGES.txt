GRAVITY GRIND - Changes from the design document (one-pager)

KEPT FROM THE PLAN
- 2D physics ball racer with two kinds of courses: 3 tabletop tilt mazes and 3 side-view courses.
- Tabletop: tilt the board with the mouse (WASD / arrows also work), avoid holes, reach the finish hole.
- Side view: A/D (or arrows) to roll, Space to jump.
- Timer, best time per course (saved), and a see-through ghost ball that replays your best run.
- Checkpoints: falling sends you back to the last checkpoint and the clock keeps running.
- Game flow: Title/Menu -> Course -> 3-2-1 countdown -> Run -> Results (Retry / Next / Menu). R restarts, Esc pauses.
- The tilt is done by pushing the ball and leaning the camera, not by rotating the physics level
  (this was the plan in the "Concerns" section and it kept the physics stable).

ADDED
- Medals: every course has gold / silver / bronze target times, shown as stars on the results screen and menu.
- Time bonus pickups: stars (tabletop) and coins (side view) take 1 second off your time.
- Tabletop extras: keys and gold lock blocks, spinning bars, angled corner wedges, boost pads,
  portals (teleport pairs), pinball bumpers, trapdoors that open and close on a timer, and narrow
  bridges lined with holes.
- Side-view extras: springs, spikes, one-way platforms, slippery ice, pushable crates, key + lock, steep and
  gentle slopes, boost pads, moving platforms, an elevator, crumbling blocks, and gravity-flip fields that
  let you roll along the ceiling (with ceiling spikes and holes in the ceiling you can "fall up" through).
- Extra inspiration for the new mechanics: Super Monkey Ball (narrow bridges), Marble Madness and pinball
  (bumpers, boosts), Portal (teleporters), Sonic (speed pads), Celeste (crumbling blocks), VVVVVV (gravity flips).
- Better jump feel: jump buffering, "coyote time" after rolling off a ledge, and shorter hops when you let go of jump early.
- Camera leans with the tilt on tabletop courses so the board looks like it is tipping. Camera shake on big hits.
- Particles: dust while rolling, sparks on hard hits, bursts on spikes/checkpoints, confetti at the finish.
- The menu shows each course's best time and medal. A "Reset times" button clears saves and ghosts.

CHANGED / CUT
- Background music was cut. The project rubric asks for game sounds instead of a music loop, so the
  "M to toggle music" control was removed. The rolling sound (pitch and volume follow the ball's speed)
  is the main "satisfying" sound instead.
- The local level editor (the stretch goal in my one-pager) WAS built: Main Menu -> Level Editor.
  3 save slots for tabletop and 3 for side view, every piece from the game is in the palette,
  left click places, right click erases, Q/E switches pieces, Play Test jumps straight in and
  Esc / Menu goes back to editing. Levels save as text files on the computer, same format as the
  built-in courses. (Online sharing is still cut.)
- Courses are written as text maps in Levels.cs and built when the course loads, instead of being placed
  by hand in Unity scenes. This made the courses much faster to design and change.
- Second playtest: the key on Flip Side became a "shy key" that hops away and taunts you a few
  times before it can be caught. Slope tiles were redrawn at higher resolution so the slanted
  ground has a clean, smooth edge. Medal times were set from my own best times.
- First playtest: the courses were too short and easy, so all six were redesigned bigger and harder
  (tabletop boards went from 22x13 to 28x16 tiles, side-view courses from about 90 to 130-160 tiles long).
- Course names: Wooden Warmup, Portal Lock, Pinball Panic (tabletop); Rolling Hills, Crumble Canyon, Flip Side (side view).
